## Intent

**Problem:** A mortgage or consumer loan cannot be tracked, so net worth leaves out the household's largest debt, and a repayment matched as a transfer becomes an `internal_move` that the monthly cash flow ignores (FR9, FR33).

**Approach:** Add a `loan` account type, a liability, created with its outstanding balance and optional loan details. A transfer landing on a loan becomes `loan_payment`; balance snapshots, net worth and cash flow already handle the rest.

## Boundaries & Constraints

**Always:**
- `ACCOUNT_TYPES.loan = { classification: "liability", subtypes: ["mortgage", "consumer", "other"] }`. Web kinds `mortgage` « Prêt immobilier », `consumer` « Prêt à la consommation », `other_loan` « Autre prêt ».
- New nullable `accounts.details` JSON column (`text(..., { mode: "json" })`, as `imports.options`), typed `LoanDetails = { originalAmount: MinorUnits | null; interestRate: number | null; endDate: string | null }` in `packages/data/account-types.ts`. `interestRate` is an integer in basis points (`3,45 %` → `345`). Non-loan accounts keep `null`.
- `loanDetailsInputSchema` in `schemas/accounts.ts`: three optional texts. Original amount goes through `parseAmount` in the account currency and must be > 0; rate accepts at most two decimals, comma or dot, 0 to 100 %; end date is an ISO date. Field codes `invalid_amount`, `invalid_rate`, `invalid_date`. Details on a non-loan type: `invalid_details`.
- `createAccountSchema` and `updateAccountSchema` take optional `details`. `GET /api/accounts/:id` returns `details`. The opening balance of a loan is the amount still owed, positive, as a credit card's.
- `transferKindOf("loan")` → `"loan_payment"`, whatever the outflow account's type, as Sure's `kind_for_account`. The manual and automatic matches both go through it.
- « Sans catégorie » (`uncategorised` in `categoryCondition`, `services/ledger.ts`) excludes `isTransferSide` rather than `inAnyTransfer`, so an uncategorised loan-payment outflow counted by the dashboard shows in its drill-down. Settles the spec-6-2 entry in `deferred-work.md`.
- Create dialog: a loan kind relabels « Solde d'ouverture » as « Capital restant dû » and shows « Montant emprunté », « Taux (%) » and « Date de fin », all optional. Paramètres edits the same three fields. The account header shows the ones that are set.
- Migration generated by drizzle-kit, rebuilding `accounts` for the new check constraints, as `0009` rebuilt `imports`.

**Never:** no monthly payment or amortisation schedule, no interest entries, no term in months, no rate type, no loan subtype migration of existing data, no category for a transfer side, no change to `direction`, `countsInCashFlow` or net worth code.

## Implementation Notes

- drizzle-kit generated `0018` reading `details` from the old `accounts`, which has none; the copy selects `NULL` instead, as `0009` and `0012` were edited. A migration test checks that accounts, entries, imports and CSV mappings survive and that the dropped table cascades nothing.
- A loan always stores `details`, each field null until known; other types store `null`, so the header's `LoanSummary` renders for loans only.
- `PATCH` replaces `details` whole and requires its three keys, blank clearing a field: a partial object would otherwise clear the keys it leaves out. The Paramètres form always sends all three.
- The rate accepts a trailing `%` and is shown with two decimals (`3,00 %`), as lenders write it.
- `accountSettingsFormSchema` takes the account's currency, since the amount borrowed has that currency's decimals.
- The create dialog keeps typed loan fields when another type is picked and drops them before validation, since the API refuses details on other types.
- `transferKindOf` is a `Record<AccountType, TransferKind>`, so Stories 7.2 and 7.3 do not compile until their type names its kind.
- The « Dépenses » criterion is checked in `e2e/transfers.spec.ts` on June 2024, a month no other test uses, rather than in `dashboard.spec.ts`.

## Design Notes

Taken from Sure rather than asked: loan is a liability stored as the positive amount owed, a transfer into it is `loan_payment` from any account, snapshots are plain `reconciliation` valuations. Two departures, both from the story's wording: the opening anchor is the outstanding balance entered, not the original amount (Sure uses `initial_balance` when set), and the loan's length is an end date rather than Sure's `term_months`. Subtypes are French-market ones in place of Sure's US list. The rate uses basis points so two decimals, as lenders quote them, stay exact without a float.
